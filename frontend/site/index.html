Spot the swarm

Finds groups of accounts that share the same content far more often than chance would explain. Judged on what accounts share, never on a single post.

Synthetic data

Analyse a discussion from a link

Paste the link of a Bluesky post, a Reddit thread or a Hacker News story (review pages on Google Maps, App Store, Google Play, Tripadvisor, Yelp, G2 and TikTok are read too, without histories). Through Context.dev we read the discussion and every participant's newest public posts (on Bluesky also replies and reposts), then look for accounts that act together. Only public data, no login. The result gets its own private link and is deleted after 3 hours. A swarm is a lead to check, not proof. Bluesky and Hacker News give the whole thread; Reddit gives the comments a browser sees before “load more” (about 25). For more Reddit comments, click “View more comments” and use the saved-page card below. Reworded copies are found too (similar-text signal).

Analyse a saved Reddit page (more comments)

  1. Open the thread on reddit.com, scroll down and click “View more comments” a few times, so more comments load.
  2. Save the page: Ctrl+S (Mac: ⌘+S).
  3. Choose the saved .html file here.

We read the commenters from the page, fetch each one's newest public Reddit comments with Context.dev, and look for accounts that act together. The result gets its own private link and is deleted after 3 hours. Why save the page? A link gives the first ~25 comments; a page you saved after loading more has all of those.

Summary

This data is simulated. Results on it show that the code works, not how good the detection is on real traffic.

Signals and settings of this run

Flagged accounts

An account is flagged when it is in a group of at least 3 accounts linked by sharing content more often than chance predicts. Confidence is how much of the members' own content the group shares (0–1). It is not a probability, and a flag is a lead to check, not proof: a harmless automated feed that posts the same wire text looks exactly like a swarm.
