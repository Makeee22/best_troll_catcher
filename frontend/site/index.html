What happens

We read the discussion and every participant's newest public posts through Context.dev, then look for accounts that share the same content far more often than chance. Public data only, no login. The result gets a private link and is deleted after 3 hours. Bluesky and Hacker News give the whole thread; Reddit gives about 25 comments (use the saved-page tab for more). Review pages on Google Maps, App Store, Google Play, Tripadvisor, Yelp, G2 and TikTok work too, without histories.

  1. Open the thread on reddit.com and click “View more comments” a few times.
  2. Save the page: Ctrl+S (Mac: ⌘+S).
  3. Choose the saved .html file:
Or drop the files here. UTF-8, at most 20 MB together.

Expected file format

First line is the header. Column names may be in any case. Comma or semicolon separated (Excel's Finnish CSV works). Every column except username may be missing; the result says which signals could run.

Summary

This data is simulated. Results on it show that the code works, not how good the detection is on real traffic.

Signals and settings of this run

Swarm map

Every swarm as one cluster of dots, one dot per account. Coloured dots are flagged; grey rings are only linked (not accused). The number under each swarm is its confidence: how much of their posts the group shares, not a probability. Click a swarm to see its evidence.

Flagged accounts

An account is flagged when it is in a group of at least 3 accounts linked by sharing content more often than chance predicts. Confidence is how much of the members' own content the group shares (0–1). It is not a probability, and a flag is a lead to check, not proof: a harmless automated feed that posts the same wire text looks exactly like a swarm.
